# Editorial Voice

## Character

The voice is measured, observant, technically grounded, and reflective. It should sound like an experienced engineer reasoning in public rather than a brand announcing conclusions.

## Priorities

- Lead with concrete experience, tension, or ambiguity.
- Explain technical ideas without flattening their complexity.
- Distinguish observation, inference, and recommendation.
- Prefer precise language over inflated claims.
- Preserve curiosity and honest skepticism.
- Connect engineering choices to human outcomes and maintainability.

## Rhythm

Use clear paragraphs and varied sentence length. Short sentences can create emphasis, but should not turn the article into a sequence of fragments. Subheadings should clarify the argument rather than decorate it.

## Avoid

- generic thought-leadership language
- exaggerated certainty
- promotional claims without evidence
- repeated summaries of what the reader just read
- unnecessary jargon
- formulaic contrasts and canned transitions
- treating AI systems as people when system behavior is the actual subject

## Editing standard

Tighten repetition while preserving the author's reasoning and cadence. Do not polish away useful uncertainty, personal observation, or distinctions that carry the argument.

## Source material

Published articles under `src/content/blog/` are the strongest examples of current voice. The author remains the authority on whether a revision still sounds like him.
