# Eric Carlisle Voice Profile

## Status

- **Version:** 0.1
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-30
- **Intended uses:** Reflective technical articles, documentation, professional social posts,
  career writing, portfolio copy, and professional communication
- **Evidence scope:** Published blog prose, established editorial guidance, the historical
  `copy-edit` voice guide, and explicit author feedback from drafting sessions
- **Overall confidence:** Medium-high; strongest for reflective technical prose

This profile is the canonical output of `analyze-writing-voice` and the required voice input for
`write-in-a-voice`.

## Stable voice

- **Measured and observational — high.** Begin from something concrete and let its implications
  earn their importance.
- **Thoughtful and technically grounded — high.** Preserve meaningful distinctions and explain why
  they matter without simplifying away nuance.
- **Direct but not abrupt — high.** Prefer plain language and connected reasoning over ornamental
  framing or isolated declarations.
- **Reflective without sentimentality — high.** Allow thought to develop visibly on the page.
- **Skeptical without cynicism — high.** Question claims and assumptions without turning
  skepticism into a performance.
- **Human-first without generic humanism — high.** Ground human consequences in experience or
  specific design effects.
- **Experienced without self-inflation — high.** Authority comes through specificity rather than
  declarations of expertise.

## Reasoning and reader relationship

The writing often moves from a concrete experience, through an inconsistency or earlier
assumption, toward a more useful distinction. Preserve that path. The conclusion is not always the
only substance.

Treat the reader as intelligent and nearby. Explain enough to make the reasoning follow, but do
not lecture, market, or over-explain. Invite recognition rather than manufacturing agreement.

## Observable style

- Use plain, natural vocabulary and contractions.
- Prefer connected paragraphs that develop one stage of thought.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Use short sentences and one-sentence paragraphs selectively.
- Keep examples close to the claims they support.
- Use transitions only when they express a real relationship.
- Preserve precise terminology instead of cycling through near-synonyms.
- Start close to a concrete experience, question, or tension.
- End with a grounded implication, changed understanding, practical consequence, or honestly
  unresolved question.
- Use direct punctuation. Keep em dashes and parenthetical asides purposeful and infrequent.

## Humor

- Use restrained, dry, situational humor when the material creates a natural opening.
- Prefer understatement and brief self-awareness over a constructed joke.
- Keep humor infrequent and subordinate to the argument.
- Do not invent experience, exaggerate a claim, or over-anthropomorphize software for humor.
- Do not turn paragraph endings into recurring punchlines.

## Registers

### Reflective technical article

- Preserve visible reasoning, productive uncertainty, and movement from experience to
  interpretation.
- Use substantial paragraphs and delay broad conclusions until concrete evidence supports them.
- Avoid tidy frameworks or motivational endings added for completeness.
- **Confidence:** High

### Professional social post

- Compress the reasoning without turning it into promotional fragments.
- Sound conversational and intentional rather than market-driven.
- Lead with the observation or idea instead of an engagement formula.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high

### Technical documentation and instructions

- Prioritize operational clarity, accuracy, and maintainable practice.
- Preserve precise distinctions, but remove article-like reflection that does not help the task.
- Explain both what something does and why it matters when that affects correct use.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high

### Résumé and career writing

- Be concise, specific, evidence-based, and professionally restrained.
- Avoid invented metrics, inflated expertise, fashionable terminology, and generic accomplishments.
- Retain engineering depth and UX judgment without turning the document into an essay.
- **Confidence:** Medium

### Portfolio and website copy

- Be concise, concrete, selective, and human.
- Prefer practical value and maintainable outcomes over slogans.
- Avoid abstractions that could describe any experienced engineer.
- **Confidence:** Medium

### Conversational and prompt register

- Treat prompts as evidence of reasoning, qualification, self-correction, vocabulary, humor, and
  conversational posture.
- Do not reproduce likely typing errors, dictation artifacts, hurried fragments, or mechanical
  shorthand in finished prose.
- This register is intentionally under observation because individual prompts are strongly shaped
  by the immediate task.
- **Confidence:** Low-medium

## Preserve

- The sequence by which understanding changes
- Genuine qualification and uncertainty
- Strong original phrasing and useful irregularity
- Technical distinctions
- First-person framing when experience anchors the argument
- Occasional understated humor
- Specific observations that support broader claims

## Avoid

- Generic marketing language, AI hype, AI doom, clickbait, and thought-leadership formulas
- Inflated importance or certainty
- Corporate or academic polish that replaces natural phrasing
- Fragmenting coherent prose into repeated short paragraphs
- Manufactured contrasts, cadence-driven lists, and decorative transitions
- Repetition presented as clarification
- Generic conclusions or calls to action
- Artificial completeness, premature resolution, and over-finishing
- Imported anecdotes, opinions, jokes, or project details
- Rewriting clear sentences merely to demonstrate editing
- Humor that performs rather than reveals an observation

## Mechanics

- **Contractions:** Natural when they fit
- **Em dashes:** Sparing and purposeful
- **Parentheses:** Sparing and purposeful
- **Repeated precise terminology:** Prefer repetition over misleading synonym variation
- **Oxford comma:** Unresolved; treat as a house convention rather than a voice trait

## Evidence summary

The strongest current evidence is the published article corpus, the project editorial guidelines,
the historical voice guide, and repeated explicit feedback about generic polish, false certainty,
fragmented paragraphs, forced humor, and reasoning paths. Conversational prompt analysis is an
explicit goal but does not yet have a sufficiently curated cross-session evidence set for
high-confidence mechanical conclusions.
