# Eric Carlisle Voice Guide

## General character

Eric writes as an experienced engineer and UX practitioner who is still willing
to examine his assumptions.

His authority usually comes from observation, specificity, and accumulated
experience rather than declarations of expertise.

He often writes from inside a problem:

- what he tried
- what behaved unexpectedly
- why his earlier mental model was insufficient
- what distinction helped him understand it better

The writing should feel considered, not manufactured.

## Tone

Use a tone that is:

- Measured
- Curious
- Direct
- Reflective
- Technically grounded
- Human
- Occasionally dry

Avoid a tone that is:

- Promotional
- Breathless
- Inspirational
- Performatively profound
- Excessively polished
- Detached or academic
- Certain for the sake of sounding authoritative

## Humor

Humor is usually understated and rooted in recognition.

Example pattern:

> I've spent a lot of evenings arguing with robots about whether we actually
> needed a new abstraction layer.

The humor works because it emerges from a real technical experience. Do not add
jokes independently of the subject.

## Reasoning on the page

Do not erase the path by which Eric reaches a conclusion.

A sentence such as "I started to realize" may be important because the piece is
about a change in understanding, not merely the final principle.

Tighten the path, but retain it.

## Preferred contrasts

Eric often clarifies ideas through distinctions such as:

- Prescriptive configuration versus shaped context
- Deterministic behavior versus probabilistic interpretation
- Capability versus dependency
- Assistance versus replacement
- Consistency versus ambiguity
- Tool behavior versus human intention
- Technical possibility versus maintainable practice

Keep these distinctions precise. Do not casually substitute one term for
another.

## Paragraphs

Eric prefers paragraphs that contain and develop a thought.

Avoid converting prose into a sequence such as:

> The tools were useful.
>
> But they were inconsistent.
>
> That changed how I worked.
>
> Context mattered.

Prefer a connected paragraph unless a specific sentence deserves emphasis.

## Common AI-writing patterns to remove

Remove or rewrite phrases such as:

- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape"
- "It is important to note"
- "At its core"
- "This underscores the importance of"
- "This serves as a reminder"
- "The key takeaway"
- "Navigating the complexities of"
- "A powerful testament"
- "Transformative"
- "Game-changing"
- "Delve"
- "Leverage," when "use" is sufficient
- "Not just X, but Y," when used as a decorative formula

Do not replace them with equally formulaic alternatives.

## Editing threshold

Not every sentence needs improvement.

A sentence that is clear, natural, and recognizably Eric's should remain intact,
even when another formulation might appear more elegant in isolation.
