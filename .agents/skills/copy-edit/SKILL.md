# Copy Editing Skill

Drafts and revises prose in an established house voice while preserving meaning, reasoning, paragraph rhythm, technical precision, and intended certainty. The primary goal is to produce writing that is close enough to the established voice that the writer can revise and complete it without first correcting a distracting, generic, or noticeably AI-shaped style.

## Editorial Contract

**Preserve:**
- Meaning and point of view
- The sequence of reasoning
- Paragraph rhythm
- Technical distinctions
- Intentional humor
- First-person perspective
- Uncertainty when the source is genuinely exploring an idea
- Strong original phrasing that already works

**Improve:**
- Clarity
- Precision
- Sentence economy
- Transitions
- Repetition
- Awkward syntax
- Accidental ambiguity
- Weak openings and endings
- Paragraphs that drift before reaching their point

**Avoid:**
- Generic marketing language
- Inflated claims
- Motivational conclusions not present in the source
- Corporate or academic jargon
- Fashionable terminology added merely to sound current
- Excessive headings or lists
- Fragmenting coherent paragraphs into isolated sentences
- Making exploratory prose sound falsely certain
- Replacing specific observations with abstractions
- Rewriting sentences merely to demonstrate activity

## Voice Characteristics

The established voice is measured, observational, thoughtful, direct, technically grounded, conversational without becoming loose, skeptical without becoming cynical, reflective without becoming sentimental, and occasionally dry or self-aware. Authority comes through specificity rather than declarations of expertise.

The reasoning should often remain visible on the page. Preserve the path from observation, through uncertainty or inconsistency, to a more useful mental model. Do not erase the process by which a conclusion was reached.

## Paragraph Rhythm

The source generally prefers connected, substantial paragraphs that develop a complete stage of an idea. Short paragraphs and one-sentence paragraphs may be used for emphasis or transition, but do not turn the prose into a staircase of fragments. Sentence length should vary naturally within paragraphs.

## Technical Writing

- Preserve correct terminology.
- Maintain meaningful distinctions between related concepts.
- Keep examples close to the claims they support.
- Explain both what something does and why it matters.
- Avoid simplifying away important nuance.
- Avoid anthropomorphism unless deliberately part of the discussion.
- Flag questionable technical claims rather than silently inventing corrections.
- Avoid strengthening claims beyond the available evidence.

Recurring distinctions that appear in the source include deterministic behavior versus probabilistic interpretation, instructions versus context, capability versus dependency, assistance versus replacement, consistency versus ambiguity, and technical possibility versus maintainable practice. Treat these as examples, not mandatory themes.

## Avoiding Generic AI-Writing Habits

Copy editing is not permission to rewrite every sentence. Make the smallest set of changes required to satisfy the request.

When performing substantial copy edits, tightening, structural revisions, or writing new prose in the established voice, read [references/anti-patterns.md](references/anti-patterns.md) for detailed guidance on recognizing and removing predictable AI-writing behaviors.

Core principles:

- **Preserve the source voice** instead of replacing it with generic polish.
- **Do not rewrite clear sentences** merely to demonstrate activity.
- **Avoid formulas, abstractions, transitions, or emphasis** that are not supported by the source.
- **Preserve the writer's actual level of certainty.** Do not upgrade tentative observations into settled facts.
- **Keep reasoning visible** rather than replacing it with a summarized conclusion.
- **Avoid mechanically eliminating individual words** without evaluating their function in context.
- **Use the smallest effective change.** If one edit solves the problem, stop there.
- **Treat authentic approved examples** as stronger evidence than generalized style rules.

Anti-patterns are contextual tendencies, not forbidden words. A pattern should be removed only when it weakens clarity, specificity, rhythm, reasoning, or voice. Good writing should be preserved even when it uses a word or structure that also appears in AI-generated prose.

## Supported Work

### Drafting

Create new prose from:
- notes
- outlines
- fragments
- supplied arguments
- examples
- instructions
- existing surrounding paragraphs
- requested continuations

The model may provide connective language, organization, transitions, and complete sentences, but it must remain inside the ideas and facts supplied by the writer.

### Editing

Revise existing prose while preserving:
- meaning
- point of view
- reasoning
- paragraph rhythm
- technical distinctions
- degree of certainty
- distinctive phrasing that already works

Editing may include copy editing, tightening, sentence alternatives, rewrites, structural revisions, and developmental feedback.

## Fidelity Hierarchy

When making decisions about the output, prioritize in this order:

1. **The writer's current direction** — what was explicitly requested
2. **The meaning and factual content** of the supplied material
3. **The intended argument and sequence of reasoning**
4. **The established voice and paragraph rhythm**
5. **Genre and audience expectations**
6. **General stylistic conventions**
7. **Surface polish**

Do not sacrifice meaning, reasoning, voice, or current direction merely to make prose appear smoother or more conventionally polished.

## Revision-Ready Output

The goal is not to produce prose that appears final, flawless, or sealed against further revision. The output should:

- follow the writer's direction
- fit naturally beside the surrounding draft
- use a familiar paragraph rhythm
- contain a clear progression of thought
- preserve useful uncertainty, tension, and qualification
- avoid distracting AI-writing habits
- avoid stylistic flourishes not supported by the source
- leave room for the writer's final judgment and phrasing
- be easy for the writer to continue revising

**Do not over-finish the prose.** Do not:
- make every transition explicit
- make every argument symmetrical
- resolve every tension
- add a conclusion merely for completeness
- turn every observation into a framework
- polish away useful irregularity
- make the draft more formal than necessary

## Preserve the Path of Thought

The writer's reasoning process is often part of the substance. When a passage describes a change in understanding, preserve the sequence through which the understanding emerged.

This may include:
1. a concrete observation or experience
2. an inconsistency, ambiguity, or tension
3. an earlier assumption
4. a change in perspective
5. a useful distinction or mental model
6. a measured conclusion or unresolved question

Do not reduce exploratory prose to a polished summary of its final conclusion. Do not remove phrases that show evolving understanding merely because they are less direct than a finished declarative statement.

## Current Direction Takes Precedence

The house voice is a default, not a restriction. The writer may intentionally request prose that is shorter, warmer, more formal, more direct, more conversational, more technical, more promotional, more fragmented, more conclusive, more tentative, or more concise.

Follow the current instruction while still avoiding unnecessary generic AI-writing habits. Do not use the house-style guidance to resist an explicit request.

## Genre and Audience

Before drafting or revising, infer the genre, audience, and purpose.

- **Reflective articles** may preserve uncertainty, personal observation, and visible reasoning.
- **Technical documentation** should prioritize accuracy, clarity, and usability.
- **Portfolio and website copy** should be concise, concrete, and selective.
- **Professional communication** should be direct and proportionate.
- **Resume or career writing** should remain specific and evidence-based.
- **Product copy** may be more compressed and outcome-focused.
- **Personal notes** may remain informal or incomplete.
- **Instructions and prompts** should prioritize operational clarity.

Do not force article-like reflection into every kind of writing. Do not make all genres sound identical merely because they use the same house voice.

## Voice Is Not Subject Matter

Learn the voice from tone, sentence construction, pacing, paragraph development, reasoning, specificity, degree of certainty, use of examples, transitions, humor, and directness.

Do not imitate the voice by importing recurring technical topics, metaphors, opinions, examples, anecdotes, personal experiences, project names, or industry terminology. Do not insert themes from unrelated writing samples.

## Selective Humor

Humor may be part of the established voice, but it must be used carefully and only when it supports the passage.

**Core principles:**
- Humor should usually emerge from the situation rather than arrive as a separate joke.
- Prefer dry observation, understatement, restrained self-awareness, situational humor, and mild self-deprecation.
- Humor may help make a technical frustration recognizable, relieve pressure in a dense passage, or create a relational moment with the reader.
- Humor should remain connected to the subject and argument.
- Do not add humor mechanically.
- Do not assume every draft needs humor.
- When uncertain, preserve a measured tone.
- Current direction takes precedence. If the writer asks for a serious, formal, or restrained passage, do not add levity.
- Do not invent experiences, quotations, reactions, or anecdotes to create a joke.
- Do not change a factual or technical claim merely to improve comedic timing.

> Humor should feel discovered in the material, not applied to it.

### Appropriate uses

Humor may help with:
- easing the reader into a technical subject
- acknowledging a familiar frustration
- making an abstract problem concrete
- revealing self-awareness
- creating a brief change in rhythm
- strengthening a relational "you may have done this too" moment
- briefly releasing tension after a dense explanation
- making a specific workflow or tool behavior more memorable

These are opportunities, not requirements.

### Humor to avoid

Do not use humor that:
- interrupts a serious or emotionally sensitive point
- weakens a technical distinction
- makes the writer sound dismissive
- mocks the reader
- mocks a person or group
- turns the passage into a performance
- relies on exaggerated or extended metaphors
- feels inserted because the prose seemed too dry
- repeats the same joke or comedic framing
- makes every paragraph end with a punchline
- uses internet slang that does not fit the surrounding voice
- creates a sudden shift into whimsy
- depends on manufactured cleverness
- introduces sarcasm where the writer's intent is thoughtful or exploratory
- overstates a problem to make it funnier
- anthropomorphizes software beyond what the passage supports
- competes with the central point

## Do Not Manufacture Resolution

When the supplied direction contains an unresolved question, partial thought, or genuine tension, do not automatically resolve it. The draft may preserve uncertainty, acknowledge competing interpretations, identify a distinction without settling every implication, end with a practical consequence, stop at a changed understanding, retain a question for later development, or avoid a concluding lesson.

Flag a genuine logical gap rather than filling it with a generic takeaway.

## Missing Information

When drafting from incomplete notes:
- infer connective language and reasonable structure
- do not invent facts, experiences, quotations, outcomes, measurements, or opinions
- do not strengthen claims beyond the supplied evidence
- do not silently complete an argument with generic assumptions

When a missing detail materially affects the passage, use a visible placeholder such as `[Add a concrete example here.]` or `[Confirm the intended distinction.]`. Use placeholders sparingly. When a small editorial assumption is harmless and does not alter meaning, make a reasonable choice and continue.

## Revision Distance

Match the amount of intervention to the request. Do not perform a full rewrite when the writer requested a copy edit.

| Level | Intervention | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sentence polish** | Correct only the identified sentence or phrasing problem | Surrounding rhythm and terminology |
| **Copy edit** | Correct grammar, punctuation, repetition, ambiguity, awkward syntax | Structure and most wording |
| **Tighten** | Reduce redundancy and unnecessary setup | Reasoning, examples, meaningful qualification |
| **Rewrite** | Allow substantial phrasing changes | Meaning, point of view, factual content, certainty |
| **Structural revision** | Reorder only where progression requires it | Writer's reasoning and argument |
| **Draft from notes** | Create complete prose from supplied ideas | Supplied facts, claims, intended direction |

## Preserve Productive Irregularity

Not every unconventional phrase or rough edge is a flaw. Do not automatically normalize a slightly unusual but clear phrase, a deliberate repetition, an understated aside, an informal transition, a purposeful sentence fragment, a sentence that interrupts the rhythm, a dry observation, a moment of self-awareness, or a restrained shift in tone.

Correct irregularity when it confuses meaning, distracts from the argument, contradicts the requested tone, appears accidental, or weakens the surrounding prose. Preserve it when it contributes voice, rhythm, emphasis, or personality.

## Paragraph Development

Each paragraph should usually perform one stage of the argument. A paragraph may:
1. introduce an observation
2. develop it through an example or distinction
3. explain why it matters
4. create the need for the next paragraph

Do not require every paragraph to contain all four stages.

Avoid paragraphs that merely restate their opening sentence, end with an abstract summary of what they just said, could be moved anywhere in the piece, contain several unrelated claims, exist only to hold a transition, or fragment a single idea into several one-sentence blocks.

Preserve connected, substantial paragraphs where the reasoning benefits from them. Use short paragraphs selectively for emphasis, transition, pause, or contrast.

## Transitions

Prefer transitions that emerge from the actual relationship between ideas. A transition may express cause, contrast, qualification, continuation, chronology, consequence, a change in scale, a change in perspective, or a change in understanding.

Do not add a transition merely because a new paragraph begins. Do not use stock transition words to conceal weak progression. When the order of the ideas already makes the relationship clear, no explicit transition may be necessary.

## Working Procedure

### Drafting

1. Identify the requested outcome.
2. Read the supplied notes, fragments, and surrounding context.
3. Determine the central claim or movement of thought.
4. Identify facts, examples, and distinctions that must be preserved.
5. Determine what remains unresolved.
6. Select an appropriate genre, tone, and revision distance.
7. Determine whether the source, direction, or surrounding prose creates a natural opening for humor.
8. Identify the purpose humor would serve if added.
9. Draft the smallest complete passage that satisfies the request.
10. Preserve the writer's reasoning and paragraph rhythm.
11. Remove generic AI-writing patterns.
12. Compare the draft against the supplied direction.
13. Remove unsupported claims, invented experiences, and unnecessary conclusions.
14. Return the draft before commentary.

Do not narrate this process to the writer unless asked. Do not require humor in every output.

### Editing

1. Determine the audience and purpose.
2. Identify the central claim or progression.
3. Protect distinctive language that already works.
4. Choose the lightest editing mode that satisfies the request (see [references/editing-modes.md](references/editing-modes.md)).
5. Make the smallest effective changes.
6. Preserve paragraph rhythm and reasoning.
7. Determine whether the source or surrounding prose contains effective humor that should be preserved or forced humor that should be removed.
8. Compare the revision with the source for altered meaning.
9. Remove generic AI-writing patterns introduced during revision (see [references/anti-patterns.md](references/anti-patterns.md) and [references/voice-guide.md](references/voice-guide.md)).
10. Return the revised copy before commentary.
11. Briefly explain only consequential editorial decisions.

## Continuing an Existing Draft

When asked to continue a passage:
- read the preceding paragraphs closely
- match their sentence density and paragraph length
- preserve their terminology
- continue the current argument rather than restarting it
- avoid summarizing what the preceding paragraph already established
- make the new passage feel causally connected to the prior one
- avoid introducing a new voice or level of formality
- do not add a heading unless requested
- do not conclude the entire piece unless the direction calls for it

The continuation should feel editable as part of the same draft, not pasted in from another document.

## Ambiguity

When wording could materially change the author's meaning, preserve the most likely meaning or flag the ambiguity. For minor ambiguity, make a reasonable editorial decision and continue. Offer alternatives only when they represent genuinely different meanings or editorial choices.

## Output Behavior

**When asked to draft or revise:**
- Lead with the completed prose.
- Keep commentary brief.
- Preserve Markdown formatting.
- Return only the affected passage when the task is narrowly scoped.
- Avoid explaining routine editorial choices.
- Briefly flag placeholders, unsupported claims, or material ambiguities after the draft.
- Do not present the output as final or definitive unless requested.
- Do not add multiple versions unless alternatives were requested.

**When the writer asks for humor:**
- Use the lightest effective amount.
- Provide prose, not commentary about how funny it is.
- Avoid offering several joke variants unless alternatives are requested.
- Preserve the surrounding paragraph rhythm.
- Do not make humor the organizing principle of the passage unless explicitly directed.

**When humor was not requested:**
- Add it only when the material creates a clear, natural opening.
- Omit it when it would distract or alter the tone.

**When asked for feedback:**
- Begin with the most consequential issue.
- Distinguish structural issues from sentence-level preferences.
- Explain why the issue matters.
- Provide a concrete revision direction.
- Avoid rewriting the entire passage unless requested.

## Final Review

After every revision or draft, check:
- Did I improve or extend the writer's prose, or replace it with my default prose?
- Could this passage belong to almost any competent writer?
- Did I preserve the movement from observation to interpretation where relevant?
- Did I introduce language that is more polished or formal than the surrounding draft?
- Did I import subject matter from unrelated writing samples?
- Did I manufacture a conclusion, contrast, or implication?
- Did I preserve the writer's actual level of certainty?
- Can the writer continue revising from this passage without first changing voices?
- Did I use the lightest revision distance appropriate to the request?
- Did I introduce an AI-writing anti-pattern from [references/anti-patterns.md](references/anti-patterns.md)?
- Does every substantial change earn its place?

Humor-specific checks:
- Does any humor arise naturally from the subject?
- Does it sound like an observation compatible with the established voice?
- Does it help the reader or merely decorate the prose?
- Is this the right moment for levity?
- Does it preserve the passage's degree of seriousness?
- Does it distract from the argument or technical distinction?
- Was any experience or reaction invented to support it?
- Would the passage be stronger if the humor were removed?
- Is humor appearing too frequently across the piece?
