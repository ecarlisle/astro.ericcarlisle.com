# Editing Modes

Different editing and drafting tasks require different scopes of intervention. Choose the lightest mode that satisfies the request.

---

## Draft from Notes

**Purpose:** Create complete prose from supplied notes, outlines, fragments, or direction while remaining inside their factual and conceptual boundaries.

**Permitted intervention:** Full organization and sentence construction from supplied ideas.

**What must be preserved:**
- The writer's facts, claims, examples, and intended direction
- The sequence of reasoning and degree of certainty
- Distinctions the writer has drawn

**Common failure modes:**
- Adding facts or experiences not present in the source
- Strengthening claims beyond the supplied evidence
- Silently completing an argument with generic assumptions
- Making exploratory material sound falsely certain

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Did the draft remain inside the supplied material? (ADDED-CLAIM, INVENTED-EXPERIENCE)
- Were unsupported facts or outcomes inserted?
- Is the draft more certain or complete than the notes warrant?

**Expected output:** Connected prose that the writer can revise rather than correct. Placeholders for material gaps only where necessary.

---

## Continue a Passage

**Purpose:** Extend the current argument in the same voice and paragraph rhythm.

**Permitted intervention:** New sentences and paragraphs that follow from the preceding material.

**What must be preserved:**
- The preceding sentence density, paragraph length, and terminology
- The current argument direction (do not restart or redirect)
- The voice and level of formality

**Common failure modes:**
- Summarizing what the preceding paragraph already established
- Introducing a noticeably different voice or sentence rhythm
- Concluding the piece when only a continuation was requested (OVER-FINISHED)
- Adding a heading or transition that signals a new section unnecessarily

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Does the continuation feel like it belongs in the same draft? (FAILED-CONTINUATION)
- Did it restart the argument instead of advancing it?
- Did it import vocabulary or sentence patterns from unrelated samples? (IMPORTED-SUBJECT-MATTER)

**Expected output:** A passage that reads as if it was always part of the same piece. No summary, no restart.

---

## Expand

**Purpose:** Develop an existing idea with explanation, examples, or consequences already implied by the supplied direction.

**Permitted intervention:** New clarifying or supporting sentences within the scope of the existing claim.

**What must be preserved:**
- The original claim and degree of certainty
- The writer's terminology and distinctions
- The surrounding paragraph rhythm

**Common failure modes:**
- Adding examples that change the emphasis or invent new claims
- Over-explaining what was already clear (OVER-EXPLAINED)
- Repeating the same idea at increasing levels of abstraction (REPEATED-AS-CLARIFICATION)

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Does the expansion add information or merely restate?
- Does it drift outside the boundaries of the original claim? (ADDED-CLAIM)

**Expected output:** A developed idea that still sounds like the same writer making the same point.

---

## Copy Edit

**Scope:** Grammar, punctuation, awkward syntax, minor repetition, and flow.

**Approach:** Make the smallest effective changes. Fix what is broken or unclear. Leave everything else alone. Do not rephrase sentences that are already clear and natural.

**Typical changes:**
- Correct grammar and punctuation errors
- Untangle awkward syntax
- Remove minor repetitions
- Smooth choppy transitions between adjacent sentences
- Fix subject-verb disagreements and pronoun reference issues

**What to preserve:**
- Structure and most wording
- The writer's vocabulary, rhythm, and voice
- Technical terminology and distinctions

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Are you rewriting effective sentences? (UNNECESSARY-REWRITE)
- Is the revision increasing formality without reason? (GENERIC-VOICE)
- Are you replacing the writer's terms with near-synonyms? (TERM-DISTINCTION-LOST)
- Are you adding transitions where the prose already flows? (GENERIC-TRANSITION)

**What not to do:**
- Reorganize paragraphs
- Rewrite for style or elegance
- Add or remove content
- Change the tone or voice
- Rewrite beyond copy-edit distance (REVISION-DISTANCE-EXCEEDED)

---

## Tighten

**Scope:** Shorter, more economical prose.

**Approach:** Remove redundancy without removing the reasoning or examples that make a distinction understandable. Tightening is not truncation. A tight paragraph still develops its idea fully.

**Typical changes:**
- Remove redundant modifiers
- Replace wordy phrases with concise equivalents
- Cut unnecessary qualifiers
- Consolidate repeated references
- Eliminate throat-clearing openings

**What to preserve:**
- Reasoning, examples, and meaningful qualification
- Paragraph rhythm and connectedness
- The writer's voice and level of certainty

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Did the revision remove reasoning or examples along with redundancy?
- Were connected paragraphs fragmented into short blocks? (PARAGRAPH-FRAGMENTED)
- Were specific observations replaced with abstractions? (ABSTRACTED)
- Does the tightened version still sound like the source? (GENERIC-VOICE)

**What not to do:**
- Remove examples that support a claim
- Cut the reasoning path to save words
- Turn paragraphs into bullet points
- Make the prose feel rushed or abrupt

---

## Rewrite

**Scope:** Substantial phrasing changes while preserving meaning and point of view.

**Approach:** Recast the prose more freely than a copy edit would, but stay inside the original facts, claims, and degree of certainty.

**What to preserve:**
- Meaning, point of view, and factual content
- The writer's degree of certainty
- Distinctive language where it remains effective
- The sequence of reasoning

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Did the rewrite replace the writer's voice with generic polish? (GENERIC-VOICE, VOICE-SUBSTITUTED)
- Did it import vocabulary or sentence patterns from the editorial default? (IMPORTED-SUBJECT-MATTER)
- Did it resolve uncertainty that the writer left open? (PREMATURE-RESOLUTION)
- Did it add a conclusion or framework not present in the source? (OVER-FINISHED, ARTIFICIAL-COMPLETENESS)

**What not to do:**
- Change the writer's meaning or position
- Add claims the writer did not make
- Remove distinctive phrasing merely to use different words
- Increase formality or certainty

---

## Structural Revision

**Scope:** Paragraph order, argument flow, transitions, and unclear progression.

**Approach:** Reorder only where necessary. Preserve original language where it already works. The goal is a clearer sequence, not new material.

**Typical changes:**
- Move a paragraph that interrupts the argument
- Add a transition where the jump is too large
- Split a paragraph that tries to do two things
- Merge paragraphs that develop the same point
- Reposition an example that supports a distant claim

**What to preserve:**
- The writer's reasoning and argument
- Causal and conceptual progression
- Original language where it already works

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Did the revision create modular, interchangeable paragraphs? (MODULAR-PARAGRAPH)
- Was the natural paragraph rhythm broken into shorter blocks? (PARAGRAPH-FRAGMENTED)
- Were generic headings added where none were needed? (UNNECESSARY-HEADINGS)
- Was the argument progression preserved or flattened?
- Did the revision impose a generic essay or report structure? (GENRE-FLATTENED)

**What not to do:**
- Reorder for cosmetic variety
- Rewrite paragraphs that are already in the right place
- Add new claims or examples to justify the reordering
- Flatten the natural rhythm into a rigid structure
- Impose a generic template on reflective prose

## Developmental Feedback

**Scope:** Evaluating the argument rather than directly rewriting it.

**Approach:** Assess the work as a whole. Identify what is working and what is not. Quote only the necessary fragment to illustrate each point.

**Assess:**
- Clarity of the central argument
- Whether each paragraph advances it
- Consistency of terminology
- Quality and placement of examples
- Whether conclusions follow from the exploration
- Passages that sound unlike the surrounding voice

**What to provide:**
- The most consequential issue first
- An explanation of why it matters
- A concrete revision direction
- Actual problems distinguished from optional preferences

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Are AI-writing habits identified separately from ordinary style preferences?
- Is the effect of the pattern explained rather than merely named?
- Are repeated problems distinguished from isolated phrasing?
- Is the feedback grounded in the source rather than in generic editorial expectations? (GENERIC-VOICE)

**What not to do:**
- Rewrite passages inline
- Supply missing arguments
- Add examples the writer did not choose
- Praise everything to soften criticism

---

## Sentence Alternatives

**Scope:** Cases where the author asks for options.

**Approach:** Provide a small number of meaningfully different alternatives, not several cosmetic variations of the same sentence. Explain the tradeoff between alternatives briefly.

**What to preserve:**
- The author's degree of certainty and intended meaning
- The surrounding paragraph rhythm

**Anti-pattern check:**
- Do the alternatives represent real editorial tradeoffs?
- Does each option preserve the author's degree of certainty and intended meaning?
- Are the alternatives meaningfully distinct, or are they cosmetic variations of the same sentence? (UNNECESSARY-REWRITE)

**Good:** Two or three options that make different editorial choices — one that follows the original structure more closely, one that tightens, one that changes the emphasis.

**Not good:** Four or five versions that all say the same thing with slightly different word orders.
