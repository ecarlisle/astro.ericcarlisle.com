# Voice Guide

A detailed reference for the house voice. This guide describes how the established voice works and what patterns to preserve or remove.

## Authority Through Specificity

The house voice earns authority through observation and specificity, not through declarations of expertise. The writer does not announce that something is true. The writer shows the work: what was tried, what behaved unexpectedly, why an earlier mental model was insufficient, and what distinction helped understand it better.

The writing often starts inside a problem rather than above it. The reader encounters the difficulty alongside the writer, then follows the path toward clarification.

## Personal Experience as a Lens

Personal experience often leads into broader technical or human conclusions. The writing moves from a specific encounter to a general observation, but it does not leave the specific behind. The example remains visible as the foundation for whatever principle follows.

Do not strip out the personal framing. It is not decoration. It is the argument's anchor.

## Comfort with Tension

The writing remains comfortable with unresolved questions and productive tension. Not every piece ends with a tidy resolution. Some end with a better question or a clearer description of what remains uncertain.

Do not supply a conclusion the writer chose not to draw. Do not smooth over genuine ambivalence. Avoid over-finishing: not every observation needs a framework, and not every argument needs a symmetrical conclusion.

## Humor

Humor is understated, dry, situational, technically grounded, restrained, and occasionally self-deprecating. It emerges from recognition: the absurdity of a real technical situation, the gap between expectation and outcome, the quiet realization that something ridiculous has become normal.

Humor is not a separate performance layer. It should reveal perspective or recognition while remaining inside the subject.

### Preferred humor

- A concrete observation is usually stronger than a conventional joke.
- Understatement is usually stronger than exaggeration.
- One well-placed line is usually stronger than repeated cleverness.
- A humorous aside should not require explanation afterward. Do not explain why a line is funny.
- Do not make the surrounding prose artificially serious merely to set up a joke.
- Do not force a humorous ending onto every paragraph.
- Preserve a good original humorous line when it already works. Do not rewrite effective humor into a more polished but less natural form.
- Do not add humor simply to imitate a personality.

### Frequency

Treat humor as an occasional pressure release, not a continuous register. A longer article may contain only a few humorous moments, and a short passage may contain none. Humor should generally remain low-frequency.

### Distinction from subject matter

Do not borrow jokes, metaphors, anecdotes, or recurring comedic ideas from unrelated samples. Learn the humor from timing, restraint, specificity, and perspective rather than repeating content.

### Core guidance

- Do not add jokes independently of the subject. Humor works because it arises from a specific observation.
- Do not explain why a line is funny.
- Do not force a humorous ending onto every paragraph.
- Preserve effective original humor. Do not rewrite it into a more polished but less natural form.
- Keep humor brief enough that the argument remains primary.

## Preserving the Reasoning Path

Do not erase the process by which a conclusion was reached. A phrase like "I started to realize" may be important because the piece is about a change in understanding, not merely the final principle.

Tighten the path, but retain it. The reader should still be able to follow how the writer arrived at a position, not just what the position is.

## Plain Language

The voice prefers plain language over ornamental language. A direct statement carries more weight than an elaborate one. The prose does not dress itself up for the occasion.

Contractions are natural and should be preserved. The writing reads as someone thinking aloud on the page, not as someone delivering a prepared statement.

## Punctuation

The voice prefers direct punctuation. Em dashes and parentheses are used sparingly and purposefully. Excessive em dashes fragment the reading rhythm. When in doubt, use a period.

## Measured Confidence vs. False Certainty

The voice expresses measured confidence. Claims are calibrated to the available evidence. The writing will say "this seems to be" when the evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

False certainty appears when a revision upgrades a tentative observation into a settled fact. Do not do this. Preserve hedging that reflects genuine uncertainty. Remove hedging only when it represents hesitation rather than honest qualification.

## Patterns to Remove

The following phrases and patterns should be removed or rewritten. They signal boilerplate writing rather than the established voice. Do not simply replace them with equally formulaic alternatives.

### Generic transitions and throat-clearing

- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape"
- "It is important to note"
- "At its core"
- "This underscores the importance of"
- "This serves as a reminder"
- "The key takeaway"
- "That said" — when used to reverse direction without content
- "Needless to say" — when the opposite is true

### Marketing and hype language

- "Transformative"
- "Game-changing"
- "Revolutionary"
- "Groundbreaking"
- "A powerful testament"
- "A robust solution"

### Jargon dressed as insight

- "Navigating the complexities of"
- "Leverage" when "use" is sufficient
- "Delve"
- "Unpack" when "explain" works
- "Drill down"
- "Circle back"
- "Deep dive" when "look at" works
- "Actionable insights"

### Decorative constructions

- "Not just X, but Y" — when used as a formula rather than a meaningful contrast
- "More than just" — same problem
- "It's not about X, it's about Y" — same problem
- "The reality is" — implies the reader has been misled
- "The truth is" — same problem

### Stacked modifiers

- "Truly unique"
- "Very unique"
- "Extremely important"
- "Incredibly useful"

## Preserving Precise Terminology

The house voice maintains careful distinctions between related concepts. Terms such as instructions and context, capability and dependency, ambiguity and inconsistency, deterministic and probabilistic are not interchangeable. When a revision substitutes one of these terms for another, it collapses the distinction the writer intentionally preserved.

Keep the writer's terms. Repeat the precise word when consistency matters. Do not introduce near-synonyms for the sake of variety.

## Avoiding Abstraction

The writing earns its conclusions through concrete observation. When a revision replaces a specific event with a general principle, it removes the evidence the reader needs to follow the reasoning.

A sentence like "The tool returned different results on the same input" should not become "This reflects the complexity of modern systems." The original describes something observable. The revision describes a category.

Keep the concrete event visible. If an abstraction serves the argument, it should follow from the specific rather than replace it.

## Revision-Ready Prose

The goal is not to produce prose that appears final. The output should follow the writer's direction, fit naturally beside the surrounding draft, use a familiar paragraph rhythm, preserve useful uncertainty and tension, avoid distracting AI-writing habits, and leave room for the writer's final judgment.

Do not over-finish: do not make every transition explicit, resolve every tension, add a conclusion for completeness, turn every observation into a framework, or polish away useful irregularity.

## Visible Reasoning

The reasoning path is part of the substance. Preserve the movement from observation to interpretation. When the writer describes a change in understanding, keep the sequence: what was observed, what was assumed, what changed, and what resolved or remained open.

## Paragraph Continuity

The house voice prefers connected paragraphs that develop a complete stage of an idea. Avoid fragmenting coherent prose into a staircase of short or one-sentence paragraphs. Use short paragraphs selectively for emphasis, transition, or pause.

## Productive Irregularity

Not every unconventional phrase is a flaw. Preserve deliberate repetition, understated asides, informal transitions, purposeful sentence fragments, dry observations, and moments of self-awareness. Correct irregularity only when it confuses meaning, distracts from the argument, contradicts the requested tone, or appears accidental.

## Selective Incompleteness

The writing does not need to resolve every question. A draft may preserve uncertainty, acknowledge competing interpretations, stop at a changed understanding, or retain a question for later development. Do not automatically supply a conclusion the writer chose not to draw.

## Genre Adaptation

The house voice adapts to context. Reflective articles may preserve uncertainty and visible reasoning. Technical documentation should prioritize accuracy and clarity. Portfolio copy should be concise and concrete. Professional communication should be direct and proportionate. Instructions should be operationally clear.

Do not force article-like reflection into every kind of writing. Do not make all genres sound identical.

## Voice vs. Subject Matter

The voice is expressed through tone, sentence construction, pacing, paragraph development, reasoning, specificity, degree of certainty, use of examples, transitions, humor, and directness. It is not expressed through recurring technical topics, metaphors, opinions, anecdotes, or project names.

Do not imitate the voice by importing subject matter from unrelated writing samples.

## Compatibility with Surrounding Prose

When continuing or revising a draft, match the surrounding sentence density, paragraph length, terminology, and level of formality. The new passage should feel causally connected to what precedes it. Avoid introducing a noticeably different voice or restarting an argument already underway.

## Editing Threshold

Not every sentence needs improvement.

A sentence that is clear, natural, and distinctive should remain intact, even when another formulation might appear more elegant in isolation. The goal is not to make every sentence the best possible version of itself. The goal is to make the prose work as a whole while sounding like the same person wrote it.

When in doubt, leave it alone.
